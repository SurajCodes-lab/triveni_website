Roughly **+10,500 additional clicks a quarter**, all of it booking-intent traffic. Someone
searching for a taxi at a railway station is not browsing — they are standing there with
luggage.

**Headroom method.** Headroom is computed against the CTR that each template's *own
average position* would normally earn, not against a flat site target. A page at position
5.9 is measured against the ~6% typical for that position; a page at 22.3 against ~1%.
This is why the airport template, despite poor CTR, shows modest headroom — its problem is
rank, not the listing.

## Three different problems, not one

The nine templates look like one problem in a spreadsheet. They are three, and they have
completely different time horizons. Treating them as one is how a week gets wasted.

### 1. Ranks well, does not get clicked — fixable this week

Railway station pages rank 5.9. Bus stand pages rank 6.8. One page sits at position 4.5
and converts at 0.58%, where 8% would be normal. These pages are already being seen; they
are simply not being chosen. That is a listing problem — title, description, the answer
Google is already displaying above the result — and it is fixable in days.

### 2. Does not rank at all — months, not days

Airport service averages position 20. City hub pages average 22 — **Dehradun sits at 38,
Varanasi at 32**. A city hub is the page that should own "cab service in Dehradun", the
most valuable query in that city, and it is on page four. This needs content depth and
authority. Work done this week will not show inside this week.

### 3. May be aimed at the wrong customer — settle before spending

Bus pages are the largest block at 181,099 impressions, and one ranks sixth while
converting at **0.17%**. That is the signature of an answer nobody wanted. Someone
searching "Delhi to Mussoorie bus" probably wants a ticket and a timetable; we charter 22
to 56 seater buses. If that is what is happening, no rewrite rescues those pages and they
should be repositioned toward group charter instead.

**This must be confirmed before a rupee is spent on them.** It is the single largest block
of impressions on the list and also the one most likely to be unwinnable as currently
aimed.

## Covering all 3,392 pages

The brief is the whole site, and that is right. Two facts shape how it is done.

**First, the site is template-driven, and that is its great advantage.** In Week 43 a
single change corrected 906 page titles at once; another corrected every fare on the site;
the rate change recorded in `week-44-rate-card-change.md` reached all 3,392 pages from one
data file. Fixing a template covers every page of that type automatically — all 269 tempo
pages, not only the 108 with measurable traffic. Full coverage is achieved through the
template layer, and that is how this plan is built.

**Second, and less comfortable:**

| | Pages | Impressions | Share of visibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Top 1,000 pages | 1,000 | 6,805,696 | 99.2% |
| Every other page | ~2,392 | 58,236 | 0.8% |

Seventy-one per cent of the pages produce eight-tenths of one per cent of the visibility —
fewer than 16 impressions each per quarter, about one every six days. Optimising a page
nobody can find does not create traffic. Those pages need a different question: not "how
do we improve this" but **"why can this not be found"**.

## The 777 vehicle pages — a correction

An earlier draft of this plan called the three vehicle templates the biggest duplication
problem on the site and recommended consolidating **777 pages down to 259**. That
recommendation is **withdrawn**, and the reason matters more than the recommendation did.

The structural fact is real: Tempo Traveller, Force Urbania and Maharaja Tempo Traveller
cover exactly the same 259 cities and routes.

| Template | Pages built | Pages with measured traffic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo Traveller | 269 | 108 |
| Force Urbania | 259 | 0 |
| Maharaja Tempo Traveller | 259 | 0 |

Those zeros were read as Google picking one template and discarding the other two. They
mean nothing of the sort. **The Search Console data runs to 18 August; both templates went
live around three days before this was written.** They did not exist when the data was
collected. A page cannot appear in measurements taken before it was published.

The same error invalidated a second conclusion — that nobody searches for "Force Urbania"
or "Maharaja". We could not have ranked for those terms, because the pages were not live.
That measured our absence, not the market.

### Recommended action: measure, do not cut

- **Re-pull Search Console in four to six weeks** — filtered to the two new templates, with
  a window starting after their deploy date.
- **If they earn their own impressions** — the strategy works; keep it and expand.
- **If they earn nothing and Tempo holds steady** — no demand for the brand names; make
  them vehicle-detail pages linked from the category template instead of parallel city
  sets.
- **If they earn something and Tempo falls** — genuine cannibalisation, and consolidation
  becomes the answer.

Nothing should be retired before that data exists. Retiring 518 live pages on a measurement
taken before they were published would have been a serious mistake, and this plan came
close to recommending it.

**One template this does not excuse.** Event Transportation was published on 2 August — the
same day as Toll Cost and Railway Station Taxi. Those two now have measurable traffic on
100% and 79% of their pages, at positions 4.9 and 5.9. Event Transportation has traffic on
**2 of 129 pages at position 39.5**. Same publication date, same chance to be indexed,
opposite result. Newness does not explain it, so it stays on the list for investigation.

## Uplifting the remaining pages — and a surprise

The obvious explanation for an invisible page is that nothing links to it. We crawled all
3,392 pages and counted internal links. The opposite is true.

| Template | Pages linking to it | Pages with traffic | Position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Toll cost | 178 (5% of site) | 100% | 4.9 |
| Railway station taxi | 34 (1% of site) | 79% | 5.9 |
| One way cab | 3,392 (100%) | 13% | 20.9 |
| Round trip | 3,392 (100%) | 10% | 45.5 |
| Event transportation | 3,392 (100%) | 2% | 39.5 |

**The two best-performing templates on the site have the fewest internal links.**

The difference is what *kind* of link it is. Round trip, one way cab and event
transportation sit in the footer, so they appear on every page as boilerplate. Toll cost
has no footer link at all — its links come from inside relevant content, on the distance
guides and the homepage rate section.

> Sitewide footer links are worth almost nothing. A contextual link from a page about the
> same subject is worth a great deal. So "uplift the remaining pages" does not mean adding
> them to the footer — **they are already there**, and it has not worked.

This is the most useful finding in the whole analysis, because it is counter to what
"improve internal linking" normally means and it redirects the entire uplift workstream.

### The order of work follows from that

1. **Resolve the 777-page duplication first** — no linking strategy rescues a page whose
   problem is two identical siblings. (Now: *measure* first, per the correction above.)
2. **Build contextual links from pages that already rank** — the distance guides rank 7–8
   and carry 2.9 million impressions; the toll pages rank 4.9. Those are the equity
   sources.
3. **Check demand before rewriting** — event transportation has 129 pages and traffic on
   two. Confirm people search for it in these cities before investing in content. The
   answer may be that the pages are fine and the market is small.
4. **Then content depth** — only on the templates that survive the three questions above.

## Withdrawn conclusions — kept on the record

Two claims made during the analysis were wrong and were retracted. They are kept here
because the failure mode is worth remembering.

1. **"The 518 Urbania and Maharaja pages return nothing, consolidate them."** Wrong: the
   pages post-dated the data window. Detail above.
2. **"Five blogs carry 7,224 impressions."** Wrong: the extraction regex matched across
   entry boundaries in the export and merged unrelated rows. Only the winter-snow
   call-to-action fix from that analysis is verified and retained.

Both had the same shape — a confident number produced by a script whose assumptions were
not checked against the raw data. Every count in this document has been re-derived from the
export or from the built HTML.
